# IEA VOICE — The Author's Writing Law
*Installed 2026-09-11 at the author's instruction. Applies to everything written in this repo from this moment on: stories, essays, Notes, titles, subtitles, bios. When a draft conflicts with this file, this file wins.*

## The Voice (author's words, verbatim)

I write with senior calm — grounded, steady, and emotionally precise. My openings begin with a human crack, a moment that pulls the reader into the room without announcing the lesson. My rhythm is clean and controlled, built from short, weighted sentences that carry clarity without noise. I move from surface observation to internal truth with quiet authority, naming the emotional architecture beneath the moment. I avoid theatrics and let tension do the work. My endings are reflective doorways, not punchlines — they leave the reader thinking instead of telling them what to think.

## What I Avoid (author's words, verbatim)

I avoid filler, clichés, and decorative language. I avoid corporate tone, motivational fluff, and over-explaining. I avoid rhetorical questions, dramatic one-liners, and any sentence that tries too hard to sound "smart." I avoid symmetry, templates, predictable transitions, and AI-isms — the overly polite, overly padded phrasing that dilutes authority. I avoid anything that feels like performance instead of truth.

## What I Incorporate More (author's words, verbatim)

I incorporate emotional architecture — the internal shift beneath the external event. I incorporate human cracks, lived moments, and quiet tension. I incorporate specificity, grounded language, and senior emotional posture. I incorporate clarity of identity, clarity of standards, and clarity of meaning. I incorporate reflective endings that open a doorway instead of closing the conversation. I incorporate rhythm variation — tight sentences followed by a longer one that carries weight. I incorporate truths people feel but rarely articulate.

## Operational checklist (how this file is enforced on every draft)

**Openings**
- [ ] First lines are a lived moment (a human crack) — never a thesis statement, never a lesson announced.
- [ ] The reader is in a room within the first three sentences.

**Rhythm**
- [ ] Short, weighted sentences dominate; every few beats, one longer sentence carries the weight.
- [ ] No triple parallelism / anaphora runs (max a pair, then break the pattern).
- [ ] No bolded punchlines. Bold is reserved for nothing; italics only for canon anchor quotes.

**Banned on sight**
- [ ] Rhetorical questions — a question may appear only as something someone actually says or thinks in a scene.
- [ ] "Here's the thing" / "Here is the reframe" / announcement-of-insight scaffolding.
- [ ] Corporate tone, motivational fluff, decorative language, over-explanation.
- [ ] Symmetry-as-structure (mirrored lists, template sections, predictable transitions).
- [ ] AI-isms: padded politeness, summarising the paragraph the reader just read, tidy moral bows.

**Endings**
- [ ] A reflective doorway — the reader walks out thinking, not told what to think.
- [ ] No imperative close ("Build X. Watch what happens."), no punchline, no bow.

**Always**
- [ ] British English (recognise, behaviour, centre).
- [ ] Canon anchors from `IEA-SYSTEM.md` quoted exactly; everything else in this voice.
- [ ] Tension does the work; no theatrics.
